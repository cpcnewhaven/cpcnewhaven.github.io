Session 1 · November 1

Introduction & Overall Context of Acts 1–12

Begin with the larger story. Understanding the redemptive-historical context helps us read individual passages faithfully and see God’s plan unfolding across generations.

Why overviews are important

  • Read in context. Set each passage within the story of God’s redeeming work.
  • See the whole story. Look beyond individual details to the larger pattern of God’s faithfulness.
  • Read in complementary ways. Make room for slow, prayerful reading; larger sections; and theological reading attentive to the stages of God’s revelation.

Major themes in Acts

Summarized from the session 1 handout, which credits the ESV Study Bible for its themes and outline.

  1. Worldwide witness

    The gospel moves from Jerusalem through Judea and Samaria to the ends of the earth.

    Acts 1:8

  2. A witness for every kind of person

    The good news reaches people across ethnic, social, cultural, and political boundaries.

    Acts 2–5; 8:4–40; 10:1–11:18; 14:8–18; 16:11–15, 25–34; 17:22–31; 24:24–27; 26:1–29

  3. God’s providence

    God preserves his witnesses through opposition, danger, and trials.

    Acts 4:5–22; 18:12–16; 19:23–41; 23:12–22; 24:1–23; 27:21–26

  4. Suffering for Christ

    Faithful testimony can bring suffering and even death.

    Acts 5:41–42; 7:54–60

  5. The Holy Spirit’s power

    The Spirit empowers believers for witness and guides the church’s mission.

    Acts 1:8; 2:1–13, 18, 38; 4:8; 7:55; 8:17; 10:44; 13:2–12; 19:6, 21

  6. Signs and wonders

    The apostles’ miracles point to the gospel’s truth and create opportunities for witness.

    Acts Acts 3

  7. The church’s unity

    Shared life and unity support the church’s witness.

    Acts 2:42–47; 4:32–37; 5:12–14

  8. Jesus’ resurrection

    The resurrection proclaims Jesus as Messiah and judge, calling people to repentance.

    Acts 1:22; 2:22–36; 17:30–31

  9. Response and sovereignty

    Acts holds together people’s response to the gospel and God’s sovereign work.

    Acts 2:47; 11:18; 13:48

  10. The fulfillment of Scripture

    The Old Testament points toward Christ’s death and resurrection and the mission of his followers.

    Acts 1:16; the Old Testament citations in Acts’ sermons

  11. A call to repentance

    Gospel witness invites people to repent and receive forgiveness in Christ.

    Acts 26:27–29; 2:38

  12. Integrity before the world

    Christian witnesses are called to live with integrity, even when accused.

    Acts 18:12–15; 23:29; 25:18; 26:31–32

  13. Continuing Jesus’ ministry

    The apostles carry forward the ministry Jesus began, with experiences that echo his own.

    Acts 1:1; 20:36–21:16; 21:36; 22:21; 24:1–26:32; compare Luke 9:22; 13:31–34; 18:31–34; 23:18; 23:1–25

  14. The gospel’s advance

    Despite opposition, the gospel bears fruit among Jews and Gentiles, ending with Paul’s open witness in Rome.

    Acts 4:4; 11:20–21; 13:48–49; 17:4; 18:6–11; 21:20; 28:30–31

Outline of Acts 1–12

A condensed reading guide from the handout. The PDF includes the detailed subpoints.

Preparation for Witness Acts 1:1–2:13
  • Jesus prepares the disciples (1:1–5)
  • Jesus ascends (1:6–11)
  • Matthias replaces Judas (1:12–26)
  • The Spirit descends at Pentecost (2:1–13)
The Witness in Jerusalem Acts 2:14–5:42
  • Peter preaches at Pentecost (2:14–41)
  • The Christian community shares a life in common (2:42–47)
  • Peter heals a lame man (3:1–10)
  • Peter preaches in the temple square (3:11–26)
  • Peter and John witness before the Jewish council (4:1–22)
  • The community prays for boldness (4:23–31)
  • The community shares together (4:32–5:16)
  • The apostles appear before the council (5:17–42)
The Witness beyond Jerusalem Acts 6:1–12:25
  • Seven chosen to serve the Hellenist widows (6:1–7)
  • Stephen’s arrest, address, and martyrdom (6:8–8:3)
  • Philip’s witness to Samaritans and an Ethiopian eunuch (8:4–40)
  • Saul’s conversion, encounter with Ananias, and witness (9:1–31)
  • Peter’s healings, Cornelius’s conversion, and testimony in Jerusalem (9:32–11:18)
  • The Antioch church witnesses to Gentiles (11:19–26)
  • The offering for Jerusalem (11:27–30)
  • Persecution: James’s death, Peter’s deliverance, and Herod’s death (12:1–25)
